
From 1978 to the present, Fyddin George had been transferred to the organization department of provincial communist committee for one year and three months.

He was transferred to this position mainly to examine province-wide personnel and things related to the “gang of four”, in the framework of a dedicated team of the provincial committee. The work he was in charge of was actually completed last autumn, and thence, he stayed in the provincial second reception hotel, like an idler.

There was no vacancy in Loeteau city for him. Litero Hola, deputy city chairman in charge of organization, visited him twice during travel on duty to the provincial capital, who pacified him that maybe the provincial committee had another assignment for him in the future, so he should wait on. Mr. Victor German also visited him once, but this time, old mister German was very politely asking him if he had any opinion to the transfer. What else should he answer? He only replied that he would completely follow the order, without any special requirement. After Mr. German left, for a long time, he couldn’t understand why Mr. German turned to be so polite. Anyway, he was comfortable to see this attitude, because what he wanted only was Mr. German could treat him as comrade or colleague in future works, whatever he was going to do.

For the year and more, he was staying in a bungalow of the reception hotel. He had not returned home, except during Chinese New Year, he lived in Weston county town for about 10 days. His wife Clove and his daughter Brila visited him once in last year, but they left one week later, due to busy work of county hospital.

When there was no job to do, he usually lay abed to read books in the dormitory. That was a wonderful chance for reading. Books were everywhere, on his desk, on windowsill, on his bed; there were books of all kinds, ancient, modern, historical, literary, geographic, Chinese, foreign... He was also too lazy to sort them, so the books were stacked in mess— anyway, there were rare visitors to him, and it was only a leisure place for his temporary stay, so he didn’t care much.

Most of the books were borrowed by his son Matin George from university library, who was a teacher in the university, after graduation from the same university. Before graduation, his son had been studying hard mainly on classic literature, and had already published several academic articles on the university periodicals, though as a recommended student from farmers, workers and soldiers. He treasured the issues of periodicals containing his son’s articles, and put them under his pillow always, and took them out to read frequently, so he could almost recite the articles. He was quite proud of his son, yes, his son’s articles! It seemed to him that his son was a child yesterday but today had published articles! Moreover, his son’s articles were hard to understand anyhow— he still remembered that he taught his son the first letters!

Matin George had been in a poor health condition before 6 years old, often suffering from tracheitis and tonsillitis, and often getting a fever up to 40 degrees centigrade, accompanied by convulsion. Although Martin’s mother was a doctor, she was always frightened to tears. Ah! For this kid, how many sleepless nights his wife Clove and he had spent! The couple usually sat on bed, take alternatives to hold the kid in arms; for only one night, the kid’s vomits could dirt all the bedclothes— in those nights, how could Clove and he imagine that one day their son would have published profound articles? At that time, what they expected from this kid was growth, because a grown-up boy would have a healthier body with stronger immunity...

Thinking of these, Fyddin George would become wet-eyed, sitting abed for half a day, on his person. Whoever would treasure its own child like a piece of flesh in his heart tip. He felt warm that his son became to take care of him now. Whenever visiting him, his son would take some nutritious foods, and had bought him a electric cup to boil water in the fear that the hot water of hotel was not boiled up. The happiest time was when he discussed with his son seriously. Good boy! His son learned to debate with him like a reasoning adult. ###Great! Hope that you can become a greater man than your father! However, my son, please take care in public, when you are speaking! You may have known this...###

When he thought of his son, he would also thought of his daughter Brila who was in a completely different character with whose elder brother. Matin was quiet, calm, while Brila was noisy, boyish. She was very healthy in childhood, almost never suffering from any disease, and had grown up without notice. This girl was born active, smart, and weird. He remembered that when she was 6 years old, her parents carried her to live in the provincial capital for days, and one day, after they went to a zoo, she suddenly asked, “Dad, which is the cruelest animal on the earth?”

He replied indifferently, “Tiger or lion.”

She raised her face, and argued, “No!”

Her mom then asked her, “Which animal do you think is the cruelest?”

She answered, “Human being!”

The couple was stunned, and then mom asked further, “How can you compare human beings with animals?”

Unexpectedly, she reasoned, “Hasn’t dad said human being is a kind of advanced animal?”

Sure, he had told her so. Then he asked his daughter, “Why do you think human being is the cruelest animal?”

She answered, “Look! All animals are kept in cages, and even the cruel tiger is caged. Isn’t human being the cruelest?”

Clove and he the couple couldn’t argue her down.

So far, he had always remembered that argument, and had tried to guess what curious brains did this girl have, but after all, he would never be clear what a woman would this girl grow to be in the future.

Now, his daughter was already grownup, nearly 21 years old. She had graduated from high school, but failed the entrance exams for universities, in short of a few points of results. Now, she was studying for another try. He knew that the cultural revolution had delayed this girl’s development, and given a normal environment, she should have succeeded in the entrance exams for universities, because of her talents. However, he still had some hope, for he knew this girl had some stubborn energy. Yah! With such stubbornness, sometimes, she even disregarded her parents! In recent years, he was increasingly concerned about her personality. Her character was too rebellious, and her passion was too aggressive! He remembered that when she was in primary middle school, she had disturbed his cupboard of books into a mess, and she read every book he stored. Since an unknown time, she had began to read his Reference News, and debated with him on dining table, while some of her opinions about international issues were quite surprising to him. For example, once a time, she shew compassion to Israel, and when he rebuked her, she argued, “Never try to change my mind! The Jews have suffered so much in the Second World War, persecuted, killed, that’s why I’m compassionate to them!” Maybe she had read some books about Jews in WWII, but since she had mixed Jewish hardships in the past up with Jewish expansionism at present, he chose not to argue then.

Of course, he was loving and caring his daughter very deeply in his heart, because she was full of compassion and integrity, except that her character and passion were too hot, though her rationality was sound. Although her one-sidedness was due to young age, this was better than stupidity and brainlessness. How much did he expect that she could finally be enrolled in a university, to receive higher education...

Fyddin George curled on bed, reading books, in the reception hotel. Whenever he finished reading and took a rest, he couldn’t help thinking of his children. The majority of his life was spent in busy work, rarely did he have so much free time to happily think of his family.

Was he too sensational?

But who wasn’t so emotional on the earth? Just because of heavy work and hard business, people usually concealed their emotions in the deep inside of soul, but such emotions were not lost. Nope! Such buried emotions were always more profound, and more immense!

Fyddin George did hardly anything other than reading, when he was jobless. He went out scarcely, and visited friends scarcely, however, at times he went to provincial writers association, and chatted with an old writer senor Black. Fortunately, the writers association was nearby, almost next door, so he went there by walk. In addition, senor Black had a rich storage of books, where he could borrow some books he loved— senor Black had never lent books to other one than him. He was the only exception. Senor Black’s full name was Yoke Black (he knew this later), who renamed to Black&White after being a writer (Look! A writer’s pseudo was always weird). In 1958, he worked as vice director of Loeteau city government office, and became good friends with senor Black. At that time, he was only 25 years old, while senor Black—then called old Black, was already 43 years old, they became young and old friends. One year before he graduated from the People’s University of China, and returned to work in Loeteau, Black&White had been a part-time vice-mayor of Norton county, whose main job was to experience the rural life and write a long novel to depict the cooperativization of mountainous areas (later, the book involved the “great leap forward” and the people’s communes). At that time, Fyddin George was deputy director of logistics in Loeteau city government office, and always visited Black as the representative of city committee and city government, urging Norton county officials to take good care of Black’s life. Whenever senor Black returned to Loeteau city, he would lodge him in the best room of hotel, and arrange that cars of the city government could be called up by senor Black at any time. In the critical stage of senor Black in writing the long novel, he decided to relocate senor Black to Loeteau Hotel for intensive writing. Thence, they become close friends, who could talk about all topics. That novel was later published in the name of “The sun is right overhead”, winning great influence. In 1959, senor Black returned to the provincial writers association, and in the subsequent years, he would visit senor Black every time he traveled on duty or for meeting, to the provincial capital...

Now, two decades passed, Black&White was already 63 years old, and then old Black turned to be current senor Black, while he himself was 46 years old, turning from then young George to current old George, but their friendship was as mellow as before, and they could always talk about all topics. Currently, senor Black’s main topic was “cultural revolution”. From the beginning of “cultural revolution” to the collapse of “the bang of four”, in a ten-year span of time, senor Black suffered a lot of tortures and hardships. Senor Black joked that the campaign had confused “black with white”, but now, the truth had distinguished “black from white”...

Sometimes, Fyddin George was annoyed too, not in a mood for reading book or visiting senor Black, then he would took on that black cloth cotton-padded jacket, unbuttoned, with arms out of sleeves, and walked back and forth in the woods behind the reception hotel. His annoyance was, currently when the nation was in a critical point of turning into an exciting period, he was idling about here, jobless. When should he be assigned a job? Letra guessed that maybe the provincial committee would employ him in the provincial capital, but he disliked to stay here, yet willing to go back to Loeteau city. Ah! If only he was back, he’d rather even serve as an assistant to Promo Lee in Weston county. What he desired now was not a position, but a job. But what was the view of Mr. Victor German, who visited him in a dramatically changed attitude, however, Mr. German didn’t mention anything about his job, other than his opinion on this current idle “job”. How dared a communist require the communist party to offer a job at his will?

He walked back and forth in the woods, not knowing what a future was ahead of him.

Then continued waiting...

This afternoon, when he was walking about in the woods, someone was walking toward him, Leef? Wasn’t it Leef? Why was she coming here?

But it was indeed Leef.

She was now standing in front of him, saying, “I come here anew, just now, I tried to find you in your hotel, but your door was locked, and when I asked the servants, they told that you’re walking about here...”

“What are you coming for?” He asked his niece, while leading her back.

—“I’m transferred to the children department of city committee for communist youth league. Before leaving Weston county town, my second aunt asked me to visit you here, and send you some seasonal clothes... After transfer, I have a few days of leave, so I come here by bus...”

“Have you dined?”

—“Yes! I ate some food right after arrival.”

“Wait a minute at may doorway, I will register a room for you...”

After registering a room, Fyddin George returned to his residence quickly. The door was still locked, and Leef was standing still at the doorway, before a big luggage.

He opened the door, and let his niece in, busy making basin water for her to wash dusty face, and making tea for her to relieve thirst.

Leef asked him to sit down, not to do so much, and quickly, she made tea for him before he did.

When she was washing her face, Fyddin George asked, “How were you transferred to the city committee for communist youth league?”

—“With the help of Lily and her boyfriend.”

“Lily Tulip, daughter of Sage Tulip? Isn’t she your former classmate, isn’t her dad deputy director of city culture bureau? Why did they transfer you to the city youth committee?”

—“Mainly with the help of Lily’s boyfriend,” clarified Leef.

“Who’s Lily’s boyfriend?”

—“William Martin, a leader of city youth committee.”

“He is not director of labor and personnel bureau, too young...”

—“His father is director of city personnel bureau.”

“Oh...” Fyddin George now remembered Sicuro Martin, deputy director of city personnel bureau— maybe that William Martin was Sicuro Martin’s son?

Fyddin George didn’t speak for a great while. Although Leef was transferred through interpersonal relations, he was not going to rebuke her, for he knew Leef was not living happily with her husband, the marriage was a tragedy, and she was not going to stay in Weston county. He was supposed to help her change a job, but his own job was at doubt, how could he help her? Well, the current situation was also fine, Leef was grownup, and it was a good thing for her to manage her own future.

Fyddin George in such a short time sensed that his niece was somewhat relieved from misfortune, or at least resumed normality in appearance. How much had he be concerned that she would become lunatic.

But Fyddin George also pitied on Avant and Promo, the two persons, who were also unlucky, in particular Avant— a good boy. Ah! How could this boy so stubborn to favor Leef? That young man, ah, was really crazy! Knowing it was a tragedy, he jumped in voluntarily! There was no way out, except as he had thought before, time would solve their problem..

Fyddin George didn’t mention Avant and his family, in order not to hurt Leef. He only asked for this own family affairs, and encouraged his niece to study on the new job, and increase her competence— because she must be unaccustomed to the administrative job very much, due to zero experience in it...

Leef stayed here for days, cleaned all his clothes, and made up lost buttons for his clothes. He called up Matin here, and took Leef and Martin to feast at a famous restaurant. On the fourth day, Leef departed for Loeteau, and before she departed, she tidied his room and sorted his books in place.

The third afternoon after Leef’s departure, Fyddin George went to the provincial writers association to return the borrowed books to senor Black, and borrowed some new books from the same.

When he returned to the reception hotel, he found a sedan car parked at his doorway, and his door was opened too. Not knowing what happened, he walked forward immediately. Not far from his doorway, the director of reception hotel caught up with him, and said nervously to him, “Aya! Where were you just now? Quick! The provincial communist committee chairman Mr. Arc and vice chairman Mr. Stone are waiting for you in your room!”

Fyddin George’s brains exploded, ”Bad luck!”, thinking so, he walked hastily to his dormitory.

Hotel servants were making teas for Harvest Arc and Bell Stone. The two provincial officials stood up ready to shake hands with him, as he was approaching.

Bell Stone told him, “After Chairman Mr. Arc visited several old experts at provincial archeology research institute, he asks me to bring him here, to visit you...”

Harvest Arc, holding a cup of tea, glanced at him, and spoke, “Are you Fyddin George? We are old friends!”

Fyddin George was somewhat surprised, for he had forgotten where and when he met Chairman Mr. Arc. No! How could he become an old friend of Chairman Mr. Arc?

So he had to reply, “Chairman Mr. Arc, you must make a mistake...”

“No! No!” Harvest Arc smiled, “We have not seen each other before, but indeed we are old friends! At least, I have known you since very early. In 1957, when I worked with the Ministry of Agriculture, I managed internal periodicals for a period of time, and at that time, there was a student named Fyddin George at the Department of Planned Economics, the People’s University of China, who had published several high quality articles on the periodicals. For some of these articles, I wrote editorial notes. Was that Fyddin George you?”

Now, Fyddin George understood. He was very moved, “Yes! At that time, I didn’t know the details, I cannot imagine that you still remember me after so many years!”

“I have recalled that. Moreover, I remember that I also asked somebody in charge of human resources to look for you at the People’s University of China, wanting you to join the Ministry of Agriculture after graduation, but you refused resolutely, saying that you would go back to Loeteau, so we didn’t force you, as I am Loeteau native too! Glad to reserve some talents for our Loeteau!”

“I remember now! At that time, the Ministry of Agriculture of Central Government did interview me,” recalled Fyddin George.

After servants retreated, the room was occupied by only the three men.

Harvest Arc sat on the edge of his bed, asking, “Which county of Loeteau city are you from?”

“Weston county,” replied he.

“Oh! You are from the same county with Mr. Budget Alto. We have a local proverb in Loeteau: Weston girls, Easton boys. That’s why I have a Westonese wife!”

The three of them laughed aloud.

“Senor Alto visited Weston the year before last year,” added Fyddin George.

“I see,” said Harvest Arc, “after back to Beijing, senor Alto came to my home and chatted with me for half a night, talking about the poverty of our hometown, he was crying tearfully... Oh! Mr. Fyddin George, will you please talk about how to change the poor and undeveloped conditions of Loeteau city?”

Fyddin George turned stupid at such a sudden and sharp question of provincial chairman.

He thought it over, and answered, “The most urgent must be rural problem. In my opinions, the first step is to generalize the sub-teaming system of production responsibility, or even contracting production to families in some places. These methods have already been practiced in Verrmi province and Wome province, very successfully! Why can’t we practice? If it’s proven that contracting to families is effective in undeveloped mountainous areas, then we can generalize this manner in total production responsibility system...”

“The problem is, if the collective means of production disappears, how can the superiority of socialism be represented?” Bell Stone interrupted, in a tune not going to object, but suggestive of a more convincing demonstration.

Fyddin George argued boldly, “Slavery was also a collective means of production!”

Harvest Arc and Bell Stone laughed.

Fyddin George was embarrassed and stopped argument.

At this moment, Harvest Arc seriously talked to him, “Mr. Fyddin, the provincial communist committee has decided to promote you to mayor of Loeteau city government. Hope that when you are back, you can quickly campaign a new working atmosphere... Later, Mr. Bell Stone will talk with you in details.”

Fyddin George was stunned.

He immediately replied to the two provincial committee chairmen, “Such a heavy duty, I’m afraid I’m not so competent to undertake. May you reconsider...”

“Decided! Get prepared, and return to Loeteau as soon as possible. Don’t retreat again!”

Encouraging so, Harvest Arc stood up. The two chairmen shook hands with him and left.

Fyddin George sent off the two provincial committee chairmen, and walked back to his dormitory soon. He shut the door, stood on the floor or ground, tilting his grey-haired head, beginning to ponder on how to fulfill this new mission.

Next day, the off-road car, after bumping over 4 hours or more across the northern hilly roads, entered into Eastive of Loeteau river around noon—although the road surface became wider, cars on the road became thicker, so the off-road car had to slow down.

Looking from the window, the vast Eastive was in desolation. It was around the beginning of winter, crops were all reaped, the farmlands were clear from even straws. The hills afar was deprived of greenness, grasses and trees withered, the ground was bare, the sky was gray, misty, yellow leaves were flying between the heaven and the earth.

According to past experience, the cold high pressure from Arctic Ocean and Siberia would carry the cold current overwhelming the entire Loess Plateau, then the real winter would come...

The car soon came in the Eastive industrial area. Through the smokes, one could vaguely identify the nine-storied ancient pagoda or tower afar and the yellowish glitter of sunset on the tiptop.

The car moved carefully on the road surface between a block of plants, in low speed. The roars of machines, the metallic sharp beats were from all directions; many materials and articles were stacked on the roadsides.

A few new stylish buildings stood out from a large area of inclined, weathered, shaggy houses...

Fyddin George, sitting in the front seat of the sedan car, had already shifted his mind from rural problems to industrial problems, unintentionally. There were more industrial problems than agricultural problems across the city. For example, leather gloves, leather jackets among other traditional bestsellers now found a hard way to sales.

Without quality improvement, but lowering quality, how could they compete in the changing market? Currently, there was no solution to this difficulty. Industry was unlike agriculture, and from a nationwide perspective, it was impossible to make any radical industrial reform at present. What he could do now was merely make careful rectification in terms of business management and labor discipline...

Although Fyddin George had some different opinions with former city chairman Mr. Victor German, he realized that his predecessor’s ideas and practices on city-wide industries were generally correct.

South coal, north petroleum, middle textiles /light industry, and tobacco /cigarette, formed the four pillar industries of this city. Thanks to Mr. Victor German who had laid a good foundation of Loeteau industries.

The current problem was he should not be satisfied with status quo, instead, he should expand and develop in a large scale. He had already started to do this quickly. Petroleum production capacity was originally 80,000 tonnes per year, and he was building a new refinery plant of a yearly production capacity of 150,000 tonnes. After bargaining with the ministry of chemical industry and provincial authorities, the plant was exempt from taxes for the first threee years, so all of its profits could be utilized by the city; millions of yuan per year, this was a very considerable amount of money for the city to be financially autonomous in the following years.

In addition, last month, he and mayor Litero Hola flied to Beijing, and made a friendly yet difficult negotiation with the ministry of coal. Finally they signed a contract, to build a mine in Souton county, with annual production capacity of 210,000 tonnes coal, which would be invested by the central government and constructed by Loeteau city government.

Local cigarette was originally a very advantageous industry, but the central government had restrictions, so it was impossible to develop it in large sale...

In Fyddin George’s minds, there was a greater dream haunting: railway connection to Loeteau, within his term of office! Of course, this was an unimaginably difficult dream, but he wanted to make all efforts realizing this dream. He reckoned that after some major agricultural and industrial problems were combed, he would lead a delegation to visit Beijing and meet some top officials. He had written to senor Alto and many other senior officials working in the central government who were natives of Loeteau, and they all replied positively, suggesting that he should take some local specialty products so that they would hold a small tea party at the Parliament Hall, which would be attended by some “key” persons, one or two of whom might be executives of the Politburo...

When the car moved in the urban area, Fyddin George saw everywhere was cleaners on the streets— various organizations were “clearing doorway snow individually”, clearing trashes, muds and dirts from the sidewalks in front of buildings, while a red ribbon slogan tied propagating vehicle was moving in a beetle’s speed, whose eardrum-piercing sharp sounds from loudspeakers were broadcasting the notice of city committee and city government to rectify the urban orderliness and sanitation.

Fyddin George was very satisfied with this atmosphere, frankly speaking, it was too filthy in Loeteau city, so the city should be necessarily in such a great maneuver to reshape the city image. However, he was afraid that this might turn out to be another “campaign”, too political to be true, and afterward it might turn back to its old dirty image.

Well, quite probably! Back to the city committee, Fyddin George didn’t go back home, though it was a few yards away, behind a small gate, but to him, these two nearby places were two long apart places. Clove always complained about his ignorance of home, saying to him, “official position” was temporal, but home should be eternal.

“Eh! Maybe you’re right, but I cannot suffice for two!” He really admired some one who could balance the onerous official duties with easy private life, but he was not good at such. In his deeper soul, he sometimes also admired some general officials and common workers who could go to work and get off work on time, spend free time watching TV, listening to musics, and enjoying the life with kids. But he? Running around for seasons, back to office, working day and night. Even when he was home, the home wasn’t at peace, full of guests who were queuing in the living room, waiting for a “personal meeting” with him. Sometimes, he had to eat his meal before the eyes of a crowd. He remembered that one provincial vice chairman said that a city chairman was like a lieutenant— meaning that this position was of light duty. “Hey! Be a ‘lieutenant’ and you will see!”

Fyddin George went back to his office and found there were a hill of files stacked on his desk— some of which were signed by him!

He stood still before this “hill”, sighed, and then went to toilet to wash his face. Then he sat in place, in a mood of “galant old man daring to dig out the hill hoe by hoe”, ready to review these files, when executive vice mayor Toler Drift opened the door and came in carefully, the former higher official being somewhat timid before him.

Fatty Toler untied his thin camel color neckerchief, and said, “I heard you are back just now...”

Fyddin George sat with him in the sofa, and replied, “I’m right from Weston.”

Hearing the word “Weston”, Toler shew a nervous face, maybe he thought of past unhappiness as colleagues. But generally speaking, with time and change, the two men should be okay to work together, as the new jobs or positions would further their mutual understandings.

In the eyes of Fyddin George, many shortcomings of Toler Drift were because of mindset or methodology. This old graduate of junior normal college was diligent on duty indeed, very careful in doing everything, including some wrongdoings. Since his taking office in Loeteau, Toler always supported him, unexpectedly. Therefore, they began to build a new relationship of trust, which was precious!

The social transformation forced Toler Drift to realize the past practices didn’t work at present. He was educated, reading and learning could help him getting rid of past burdens that were outdated, although he was short of breath, he wanted to catch up with the epochal pace. However, he reserved some opinions on Fyddin George’s ideas to some extent, anyway, he realized that this man was open-minded, tolerant, and forgiving, who after taking power was not revenging him for his past hostility; instead, who always trusted him and persuaded the provincial government to make him executive vice mayor. To this end, Toler Drift tried his best to be a good assistant to Fyddin George.

Sitting down in sofa, Toler directly complained to Fyddin George, “Clever White the man is too arrogant!”

“What’s wrong?” asked Fyddin George.

—“The city committee and city government are both fined 200 yuan!”

“For what?”

—"Said our sanitation project is not well done!"

"Then we should be fined!"

—“Not well done? We have followed the city standards, and suspended our work for nearly two days, to motivate our officials to clean up, actually, we have done better than other organizations, but Clever White led the inspection team around and said our sanitation were not well done, and we must be fined. A lower authority dares to manage its higher authority, isn’t it a joke?”

Fyddin George smiled, soothing, "Toler, calm down! You know Clever, who did this for his reason. He fined the city committee and city government, so that the other organizations might be afraid. We are superior authorities, that is true, but this city should be administered by the municipal authorities, you know, we should follow their administrative orders and requirements. I think, Clever is brave in doing so! I’ve noticed that a red plate of ‘health leader’ is hung on the provincial committee gate, numbered 001; a another plate is hung on the provincial military command, numbered 002. Does that mean the sanitation or health project is done the best in the highest authority?”

—"Better not play down our performance! I understand Clever’s harsh attitude, even if we cleaned with an electric vacuum cleaner, he would fine us too!" complained Toler Drift.

“I believe in your words! Toler, given this situation, why not show a high profile, say welcome to the municipal punishment on us, supporting their job?”

Toler made a bitter smile, “No way! I’d best not welcome, they can fine if they want!” Saying so, he stood up, ready to leave.

"Anything else?" asked Fyddin George.

—"Nothing else, just so. I’ve planned to ask you to call Clever so he may withdraw the punishment..."

Fyddin George was surprised that Toler visited him specially for this trifle! Hey! This man was too careful!

Before leave, Toler Drift told that a few days ago he ran into Prromo Lee, who asked if Fyddin was back or not, and wanted to talk with Fyddin.

Fyddin George nodded, "I will call him later."

After Toler Drift left, Fyddin George puzzled what Promo Lee would talk with him about? Seemingly it must be about Avant’s relationship with his niece. However, this problem was beyond the control of Promo Lee and him. They might control city problems, health bureau problems, but unable to control children’s love problem. Anyway, since Promo wanted to talk with him, he should not refuse.

Fyddin George looked at his watch, finding there was still some time for this talk, so he took up the phone from desk.

After dialing two digits, he put down the phone. He decided to visit the city health bureau on his person— a telephone call might be an official style, too bureaucratic!

When Fyddin George arrived at the city health bureau, he found that Promo Lee talked with him not about Avant and Leef’s marriage problem, but about the city people’s hospital. Promo complained that the city hospital was deadly ill too, how could it cure people?

Fyddin George felt an immediate kindness or cordiality toward this man who had been so concerned about health system right upon taking office. Only if a man worked hard, could he deserve respect. Unknowingly, he thought of his friend Smart Archer in complicated moods.

Since Promo was so enthusiastic about this problem, he should not disappoint Promo, and moreover, the city people’s hospital was the most important medical center of the city, so he should give heavy attention; he had neglected this duty from taking office to now.

Then he led Promo to find mayor Litero Hola. The three discussed, and directly took a car to visit the hospital in field at first.

After they arrived at the city hospital, all leaders of the hospital were accompanying them to tour around inside the hospital.

The problem here was really serious. Due to lack of maintenance for years, all facilities were rotten and broken, the passage on the second floor of outpatient building was overflowed by toilet discharges due to broken pipeline, so they had to step on bricks placed on the floor, like crossing a river by stepping on the peddles. The boiler was broken too, so there was no hot water for patients or doctors to drink. There were merely two telephones in the entire hospital; without even one mortuary. Although her study results were not ranking in the top, she had a solid knowledge of every subject. Pretty and Aroma were always in very close friendship, as close as born sisters. Pretty was already quite sure that she was going to apply for the candidate of provincial medical college, but Aroma was still unclear about her ideal college or discipline...

In the afternoon during extra-curriculum activity, the two good friends went together with gifts, to the county hospital to visit the hospitalized girl classmate.

At the hospital, Pretty sat on the classmate’s sickbed for a moment, and left excusing that her dad had a letter to be forwarded to an acquaintance in the county transport company so she had to forward it.

Aroma chatted with the classmate for a more while, and went out of the ward or sickroom.

Unintentionally she saw not far away from the hospital, there was a long row of cave holes being paved. She immediately remembered that second brother was doing this kind of job in Loeteau city right now.

She walked closer unconsciously trying to find out how these guys were working— so that she would get a rough estimation of second brother’s situation in Loeteau city.

Aroma walked ahead, and found all stone craftsmen or labors were all bare-armed, busy with their own things, merely wearing a small cloth collarless sleeveless shirt with buttons on their upper bodies. Some craftsmen were carving the rough rocks carefully with hammer and chisel on the clay ground; some labors were backing rock to the cave wall that was being masoned to a certain height. On the wall were standing superior major craftsmen, who were ordering the petit labors below to transport this or that. The sounds of chisels and hammers were like burning firecrackers over there.

Suddenly, Aroma found that most mortar carrying labors were young girls who were looking like urban girls, not wearing like from countryside.

So she asked one of them curiously, “Where are you all from?”

—“We are jobless urban youth.”

“How much are you earning per day?” Aroma asked boldly.

—“One yuan and a half per day.”

Ah! 1.5 yuan per day! These girls were at the same or similar age, earning so much a day!

Aroma was moved: Could I come here to carry mortar bags? Of course, she would take lessons in daytime. She didn’t know if this construction site was working in nighttime. If she could work for hours in nighttime after school, maybe she could earn tens of cents per night, which would be enough for her!

Then she anxiously walked up and asked that girl, “Do you work in nighttime?”

—“No! We are absent at nights, but these labors and craftsmen are still here working at nights,” that girl glanced at Aroma, as if Aroma were an alien.

“Then who will be carrying the mortar for them at nights?”

—“They will arrange someone of them to do so...”

“Then can I carry mortar here at nights?”

—“You?”

“Yeah!”

—“Then you may ask the headman standing on site!”

“Which person is the headman?”

That girl pointed to a man smoking a black cigar who was standing not far away.

Aroma Sun had decided to work at this site after school! She remembered what second brother told her in the letter. She thought since the US president’s daughter could earn money at free time by washing dishes at restaurant, why couldn’t she earn money at free time by carrying mortar?

Second brother was right that one should be strong and independent! All her family members were hardworking and painstaking people, did it matter that she should work a little at free time? As second brother told, one should not be afraid of toils, the more one understands the toils, the more one would feel honored by the toils. Correct! She was going to do so without any sense of shame or timidity.

Aroma was born with the brave blood of Sun family, which drove her to walk directly up to the headman and utter her wish.

The headman listened, and was greatly surprised after knowing about her background, family conditions and current situation.

Out of compassion, perhaps, the headman immediately agreed in generosity, “Okay! You can come here to work at free time without delaying your classwork or schoolwork. Working for two or three hours every night, you will be paid 50 cents!”

Aroma was pleased and excited. After she left the hospital, she was suddenly feeling that she was really grown up into an adult— she was happily surprised by her bold decision! Hard to believe!

Deciding so, she should get ready to throw herself into such kind of life fearlessly. In daytime, she could study for a longer time, and save the nighttime for work. Of course, she wouldn’t work for too long a period, because it was close to the time of college entrance exam, so she decided to work for merely one month, the pay for which would be enough for her to buy a new T-shirt. She believed that wearing a new dress with the cash earned by herself would be more meaningful! Except that she should not let the other classmates or schoolmates know this, nor her own family...

From this day on, Aroma got out of school for whatever excuse every night, and then secretly went to the construction site of county hospital, beginning to deliver mortar bags for the cave paving craftsmen.

When a beautiful girl appeared before a group of laboring men, verbal abuse or harassment was almost inevitable. But our Aroma had her own tackle: In the very beginning, she called every laboring man respectfully as “uncle” or “big brother”, elevating those vulgar guys to the nobility of ”sirs or monsieux”, which resulted in their unwillingness or shamefulness to behave vulgarly or harassingly anymore. These labors were human after all, once they were respected and moralized or honored, they would not behave beastly or treat you as an working animal.

The workload of carrying mortar bags was not light. After closing night job around 22:00, Aroma often felt unbearable pains and sores all over her body. She would firstly hide herself in women’s restroom, strip off her muddy clothes, insert them into her schoolbag, and then walked through the clear, cold and empty nighttime streets toward her school.

Walking alone on the tranquil streets, she would usually raise up her head, sparkling her pair of beautiful eyes, gazing at the immeasurable dark blue night sky, watching the bright moon and starlights around it, lost into limitless thoughts. Oh, life, universe, everything was so mysterious and incomprehensible! Suddenly, she recalled a few sentences of a poem whose source she didn’t remember: Past thousands of mountains, across thousands more of waters, still hard to access your palace...

This highly talented girl was often inspired strangely at such moments.

One night, after laboring at hospital worksite, she was walking to school, while thinking that: one day, she must drive a spaceship to travel in the outer space! Was there such a kind of college or university in China that was related to this field? She would ask her teacher about it— if there was one, she would certainly apply for that college or university!
